## Approved objectives and research questions

**General objective (§1.3.1):** Build a web platform for professional alumni networks that uses semantic vector embeddings to make intelligent mentorship matches.

1. **SO1 (§1.3.2.i):** Analyse existing alumni engagement and mentorship systems, identifying limits in scalability, matching intelligence, and institutional control.
2. **SO2 (§1.3.2.ii):** Identify the challenges in existing solutions that impede effective mentorship pairing and scalable institution-to-alumni communication.
3. **SO3 (§1.3.2.iii):** Design AlumniConnect's architecture around pgvector semantic matching, role-based access control, and full-text search.
4. **SO4 (§1.3.2.iv):** Implement that semantic engine, database-enforced role controls, and full-text search in the web platform.
5. **SO5 (§1.3.2.v):** Test matching relevance, usability, and cold-start performance using established measures.

The five research questions (§1.4) respectively ask: what current systems and their limitations are; which obstacles hinder pairing and institutional communication; how the proposed architecture should incorporate pgvector, RBAC, and search; how implementation yields cold-start-resilient matches; and whether semantic matching improves compatibility relative to conventional keyword approaches. The last question is empirical: implementation alone cannot answer it.

## Status rules

**Complete** means the specified repository artefact or implementation exists; it does not assert successful deployment or user acceptance. **Partial** means a meaningful subset exists. **Missing** means no implementation or required evidence is present in the tracked repository. **Deviated** means the current design or observed result differs materially from the approved description. **Needs Verification** means the code or claim exists but the necessary runtime, deployment, or human evidence was not established by this audit. A row may mention a secondary concern, but its status is the primary finding. Backlog IDs identify actions for incomplete required rows.

## Proposal-to-implementation deviations

These are differences from the approved description, not automatic instructions to change code.

1. **Role names and population:** §1.6 says Administrator/Alumni/Mentor/Moderator. The schema and signup use admin/mentee/mentor/moderator (`supabase/migrations/20260705000001_initial_schema.sql`, `app/(auth)/signup/signup-form.tsx`). A generic alumni participant has no explicit role.
2. **Cold-start timing:** §§1.5 and 2.5 describe profile-based matching at account creation. Signup stores a name-only profile; vector generation follows an explicit profile save and client-triggered `/api/embed` request (`app/(auth)/actions.ts`, `app/(dashboard)/profile/profile-form.tsx`, `app/api/embed/route.ts`). The two writes are not atomic.
3. **Search breadth:** §§1.6/2.5 describe independent full-text and semantic discovery of peers and mentors. The UI and SQL offer keyword search of mentors plus a personalised semantic mentor list (`app/(dashboard)/discover/discover-client.tsx`, `supabase/migrations/20260705000003_keyword_search.sql`).
4. **Model execution and component design:** §§3.5.4/3.6.3/3.7.4 describe EmbeddingService/MatchingEngine/AuthService classes, a model API and Supabase Edge Functions. The code uses functions, a locally loaded Transformers.js model, Next.js route handlers and Server Actions (`lib/embedding/embed.ts`, `app/api/embed/route.ts`, `app/api/match/route.ts`, `app/(auth)/actions.ts`). This may be a sensible architecture but needs a documented design decision.
5. **Feedback flow:** §2.5 mentions accepting, declining or deferring recommendations and future engagement feedback. The current persisted actions concern mentorship requests and their acceptance/decline, plus audit events; no defer or engagement outcome exists (`app/(dashboard)/requests/actions.ts`, `supabase/migrations/20260705000001_initial_schema.sql`).
6. **Expected research result:** The abstract anticipates semantic matching outperforming keyword/category matching. The observed synthetic category baseline ranks higher overall; the strongest semantic evidence is against skills-only keywords when vocabulary differs (`docs/eval-results.md`, `scripts/eval/run-eval.mjs`). Report the result as observed; seek supervisor guidance before changing the claim or comparison design.

## Research-validity risks

- **Semantic versus keyword-plus-category:** `docs/eval-results.md` gives overall P@1 0.833 versus 0.917 and P@3 0.806 versus 0.944. It is invalid to claim general superiority over category-assisted search. The skills-only baseline asks a narrower question. Keep both baselines and predefine the primary comparison before another run.
- **Synthetic labels:** `scripts/eval/dataset.mjs` generates profiles by domain, and `scripts/eval/run-eval.mjs` treats same-domain as relevance. The industry field can reveal this label to keyword+category; same-domain does not establish mentor suitability. No independently judged or representative alumni profiles are recorded. The proposal permits synthetic data as a limitation (§1.7.1), so the evidence should be framed as a controlled simulation.
- **Metric implementation and reproducibility:** `scripts/eval/run-eval.mjs` filters unlabelled candidates before scoring, compressing their ranks, and `pAt` divides by `min(k, 5)` even if fewer than k labelled results remain. It calls RPCs with a service-role client, bypassing normal RLS. It rewrites `docs/eval-results.md` and deletes `@eval.local` accounts. Audit these choices before treating the recorded numbers as final; do not run it against a shared or production project.
- **Cold start:** The `semantic-minimal` condition uses generated headline and skills, not a fresh signed-up user, and does not measure elapsed time, embedding success rate, no-history compatibility, or first-match availability. Empty name-only profiles return HTTP 422 at `/api/match` (`app/api/match/route.ts`).
- **SUS:** No participant protocol, task outcomes, raw SUS answers or scored SUS values are tracked. Automated checks cannot replace a representative-user usability test.
- **Role terminology:** Alumni-to-mentee mapping is an unapproved assumption. It may bias recruitment, permissions and the wording of Chapters 4 and 5 if alumni can also be mentors or neither.
- **Small, dependent evaluation:** 24 generated mentees, 40 generated mentors and a six-case vocabulary-mismatch subgroup (`docs/eval-results.md`) cannot support population claims or strong significance language. Domain-based generation and repeated templates reduce independence.

## Recommended sprint sequence

1. **Sprint 1 - Freeze claims and decisions.** Confirm the alumni/mentee mapping, institution boundary, baseline comparison, and whether the scope features may be staged. Write the requirements and evaluation protocol before changing matching logic. Preserve the current counterexample in `docs/eval-results.md`.
2. **Sprint 2 - Make the research path dependable.** Resolve first-match timing and profile input/retry behaviour; prove vector writes, RLS boundaries, semantic search and honest score explanation on a disposable local stack.
3. **Sprint 3 - Generate defensible Chapter 5 evidence.** Correct evaluation scoring and run locked comparisons; collect first-account cold-start measures and representative-user SUS/task data. Report outcomes even if the semantic approach does not beat the category baseline.
4. **Sprint 4 - Finish Chapter 4 artefacts and deployment.** Bring diagrams, API/schema reference, module tests and public smoke-test evidence into line with the implemented architecture. Record Scrum evidence that actually exists.
5. **Sprint 5 - Complete approved supporting scope.** Implement the smallest approved job referral, cohort and event workflows, or record a supervisor-authorised scope amendment. Then perform a final traceability and acceptance review. Defer excluded features.
